Homeowners across Florida, Texas, and North Carolina are searching for reliable answers about how to get an [ADU permit](/services/adu-permit-packages), especially as accessory dwelling units continue reshaping residential housing in fast-growing metros. Unlike states with a single statewide ADU mandate, all three of CADTRI's primary markets leave most ADU rules to the individual city or county, which means the process depends heavily on where the property is located.

Many projects move smoothly through the process. Others stall for months because of incomplete paperwork, missing calculations, or conflicts with local development standards. Understanding the real-world ADU permit process in your specific jurisdiction can prevent expensive redesigns and unnecessary delays.

## Understanding ADUs in Florida, Texas, and North Carolina

An ADU, or accessory dwelling unit, is a secondary housing structure located on the same lot as an existing residence. Depending on the city, these units can be attached to the primary house, detached in the backyard, or created through garage and interior conversions.

Because different configurations trigger different ADU permit requirements, homeowners should identify the type of unit they plan to build before preparing any documents for the local building department.

Four ADU categories come up most often across these markets.

### Attached ADUs

An attached unit expands the existing residence while functioning as a separate living area with its own entrance, kitchen, and bathroom. Attached additions must satisfy local zoning compliance standards, and setback and size limits vary by city and county.

Attached units are commonly used when homeowners want additional living space without sacrificing rear yard area.

### Detached ADUs

Detached units are standalone structures often located behind the primary residence. A typical detached ADU project involves new construction with independent utilities, structural framing, and separate foundation systems.

Allowable size, setbacks, and lot coverage for detached units are set locally. Florida projects near the coast, and Miami-Dade specifically, may also trigger High Velocity Hurricane Zone wind-load requirements for the new structure.

### Junior ADUs (JADUs)

Junior ADUs are smaller units created within the existing home envelope. These are frequently built from spare bedrooms, converted storage areas, or portions of attached garages.

Although JADUs are often simpler to approve than detached structures, they still require an official accessory dwelling unit permit and must comply with occupancy and safety requirements.

### Garage Conversion ADUs

A garage conversion involves converting an existing garage into habitable residential space. These projects remain popular because they reduce structural costs while maximizing existing square footage.

However, many conversions still require upgrades related to insulation, fire separation, ventilation, and structural reinforcement before final permit approval can occur. See our [garage conversion permit guide](/resources/garage-conversion-permit-guide) for the full breakdown.

## How to Get an ADU Permit

For property owners wondering how to get an ADU permit, the process usually begins long before the actual permit submission. Successful projects typically involve careful research, properly coordinated drawings, and early communication with the local jurisdiction.

### Step 1: Research Local Requirements

Because ADU rules are set city by city in Florida, Texas, and North Carolina, every jurisdiction maintains its own procedures and administrative rules. Requirements that apply in one city, such as Austin's no-parking-required policy for ADUs, may not apply a few miles away.

Before preparing plans, homeowners should verify:

- Local ADU zoning rules
- Applicable development overlays
- Utility connection policies
- Parking requirements
- Site-specific restrictions tied to the property

Speaking directly with the local building department or scheduling a preliminary review meeting can uncover issues early in the planning stage. This first phase often determines whether the project moves efficiently or becomes trapped in repeated correction cycles.

### Step 2: Prepare Permit Drawings and Construction Documents

A complete ADU building permit application requires detailed architectural and engineering documentation. Most jurisdictions require professionally prepared construction drawings before beginning the official review process.

A standard package usually includes several core components.

**Site Plan.** The site plan illustrates the existing residence, proposed ADU location, utility routes, parking layout, and distances from each property line. Cities also use this sheet to verify lot coverage, open space, and required setbacks.

**Floor Plan.** The floor plan identifies room layouts, dimensions, plumbing fixtures, doors, windows, and circulation paths within the proposed unit. For conversions and additions, plans usually distinguish between existing construction and new work.

**Structural Documentation.** Projects involving additions, framing changes, or conversions generally require calculations prepared by a licensed structural engineer. This portion of the package often contains framing details, foundation specifications, wind-load or seismic reinforcement notes (depending on the jurisdiction), and load calculations.

**Energy Compliance Documentation.** Florida, Texas, and North Carolina each require [energy code compliance](/resources/energy-code-compliance-guide) documentation for virtually all ADU projects. These reports verify adherence to the applicable state efficiency standards and must accompany the initial permit submission. Incomplete energy reports are one of the most common reasons applications fail during plan check.

## What Is Required for an ADU Permit Application?

While exact checklists vary between jurisdictions, most applications require:

- Architectural plans
- Structural calculations
- Energy compliance documentation
- Zoning information
- Utility coordination details
- Supporting engineering data

Some cities may also request drainage studies, geotechnical reports, accessibility documentation, or hurricane wind-load documentation (Florida coastal jurisdictions) depending on the scope of work. Following an organized ADU permit checklist significantly improves review speed and reduces correction notices.

## ADU Permit Process: What to Expect

After the plans are submitted, the city begins reviewing the application for code compliance, zoning conformity, structural safety, energy regulations, and life safety requirements. This review phase is commonly called [plan check](/resources/how-to-respond-to-plan-check-corrections).

Review timelines vary by jurisdiction rather than a single statewide deadline. As a general range: Florida residential permits typically take 2 to 6 weeks in major counties, Texas timelines range from a few days for simple projects to several weeks for complex work, and North Carolina residential permits are typically processed within 1 to 3 weeks for straightforward projects.

The actual review duration varies depending on staffing levels, workload, and project complexity.

## Common Reasons ADU Applications Get Delayed

Even relatively straightforward ADU projects encounter setbacks during the permit review stage.

**Incomplete Site Information.** Missing dimensions, utility routes, setback measurements, or incorrect lot coverage calculations frequently trigger corrections.

**Missing Energy Documentation.** Incomplete energy compliance reports remain one of the leading causes of delayed approvals.

**Structural Deficiencies.** Garage conversions and additions involving framing changes often require calculations from a licensed structural engineer. Applications submitted without adequate engineering support are usually returned for revisions.

**Zoning Misinterpretations.** Misunderstanding local development rules, or assuming a neighboring city's ADU ordinance applies, can create major delays. Proper jurisdiction-specific research prevents most of these problems before submission.

## How Long Does It Take to Get an ADU Permit?

The answer depends on several variables: local city backlog, completeness of the application, project complexity, and staffing availability within the reviewing department.

Simple conversions sometimes receive approval within weeks, while larger detached units requiring engineering review may take several months. Projects with coordinated drawings and complete documentation almost always move faster through the system.

## ADU Permit Cost: What to Expect

The total cost of an ADU permit varies based on location, project size, utility work, and structural complexity.

Typical expenses may include:

- Permit filing fees
- Planning review charges
- Engineering review fees
- Utility connection costs
- Impact or school district fees, where applicable
- Energy compliance expenses

Detached new construction units usually cost more to permit than interior conversions or JADUs.

## Do I Need a Permit for ADU Construction?

In nearly every situation, yes. Whether the project involves a detached structure, garage conversion, or interior addition, cities require formal approval before construction begins.

Building without permits can create serious legal, financial, and resale complications later.
